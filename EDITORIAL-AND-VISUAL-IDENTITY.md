# The Hidden Engine — Visual Identity

## Creative direction
A cinematic documentary with an investigative newsroom character. The edit should feel deliberate, evidence-led, restrained, and recognizable from its opening seconds.

## Fixed palette
- Near-black: #101113
- Charcoal: #292A2B
- Ivory: #F0EDE5
- Muted copper: #A8794D

Use black/charcoal as the dominant field, ivory for readable typography, and copper only for emphasis, rules, dates, and evidence markers. Avoid the previous teal/blue palette and avoid decorative neon.

## Image language
Prioritize relevant, identifiable material: archival photographs, historical products, company facilities, official corporate media, dated press coverage, filings, reports, and original explanatory footage/diagrams. Generic stock footage is illustrative only and must never be represented as actual footage of a named company or event.

Every third-party asset must have a source page, creator, provider, access date, and rights note recorded in the project credits. Prefer official company media and public-domain archives when appropriate; check rights individually. Do not scrape YouTube or reuse copyrighted footage without permission.

## Typography and graphics
- Short, assertive headlines; one idea per screen.
- Large dates, percentages, financial figures, and source labels.
- Simple timelines, maps, and charts built only from verified data.
- Keep layouts spacious and legible; no dense paragraphs on screen.
- Distinguish facts, attributed claims, analysis, and illustrative scenarios.

## Editing grammar
- A consistent branded opening and sound mark.
- Documentary pacing, purposeful cuts, restrained transitions, subtle movement on stills, and clear visual changes that follow the narration.
- Dark, high-contrast grade; preserve natural skin tones and product colors where factual identification matters.
- Use captions selectively and accurately; never let motion or effects obscure evidence.

## Editorial fit — mandatory gate
The channel is about the hidden economics and strategic engines of companies, not general technology news. A story must explain at least one substantial business question:
- How the company actually earns money; revenue mix, margins, pricing, or unit economics.
- The hidden economics behind a product, service, platform, or supply chain.
- Capital intensity, costs, assets, distribution, or recurring revenue.
- Competitive advantage, strategy, leadership decisions, and why they worked or failed.
- The economic mechanism behind a company's rise, decline, or transformation.

A general AI/technology story is not eligible merely because it is important or trending. It must be reframed around a documented company/business-model question. If that cannot be done with credible evidence, reject or hold the topic.

## Evidence and approvals
Use primary sources wherever possible: annual reports, regulatory filings, investor materials, official datasets, and original research. Keep dated source URLs. No invented figures, no unsupported causal claims, and no implied privileged access. Clearly mark uncertain or redacted evidence.

No video is automatically published. Owner review is required for story, script, voice, visual rights, final render, and publication.
