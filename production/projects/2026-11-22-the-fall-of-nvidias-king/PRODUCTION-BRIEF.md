## How Jensen Huang Lost the Company He Built

## The story
On November 12, 2026, NVIDIA disclosed that co-founder Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and a board member, effective immediately. The board accepted the resignation and named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO. Five days later, board chair Harvey Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate Huang after he publicly expressed willingness to return. The story is not simply a founder scandal: it is a test of board authority, national-security oversight, export controls, corporate governance and whether the world's most valuable AI infrastructure company can operate beyond its iconic founder.

## Editorial framing
Use the accurate formulation **“resigned; board says it will not reinstate him”**. Do not state that he was formally fired unless a primary source establishes that. Distinguish:
- NVIDIA's official statements and SEC filings;
- statements attributed to Huang, the board, lawmakers and investigators;
- media-reported allegations, which are not findings of guilt;
- unresolved questions about chip diversion, disclosure and national-security exposure.

No narration may present an alleged intelligence relationship, unlawful transfer, or corporate knowledge as established fact. Attribute allegations and state the status of investigations. Include NVIDIA's public position where available and note when it has not responded.

## Narrative spine
1. Cold open: the founder leaves at the height of the AI boom.
2. The official break: resignation, interim succession, board response.
3. The pressure behind the decision: reported personal conduct and national-security concerns, carefully attributed.
4. The chip question: export controls and the strategic value of advanced GPUs.
5. The return bid: Huang's statement and the board's refusal.
6. What happens next: congressional interviews, regulatory scrutiny, leadership continuity.
7. The business beneath the founder: NVIDIA's scale, data-center business and execution challenge.
8. Closing: the company is bigger than one person, but its governance is now under the microscope.
