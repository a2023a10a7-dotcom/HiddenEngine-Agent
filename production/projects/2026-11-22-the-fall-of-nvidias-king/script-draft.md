# THE FALL OF NVIDIA'S KING
## How Jensen Huang Lost the Company He Built
**Narration draft v0.1 — 22 November 2026 — Not approved for voice recording**

### 1. Cold open — The founder is gone
For more than three decades, Jensen Huang was NVIDIA. The black leather jacket. The keynote stage. The green logo. And, behind the image, a company that became central to the artificial-intelligence boom.

Then, in November 2026, the company announced that Huang had resigned as its president and chief executive officer, effective immediately.

The board appointed its longtime finance chief, Colette Kress, as interim CEO. And within days, the board made a second announcement that changed the story: it did not intend to bring Huang back.

This was not just another executive departure. It was a sudden break between one of technology's most recognizable founders and the company he had led since its creation.

But what pushed NVIDIA to this point? And what happens when a company built around one extraordinary public figure has to prove it can move on without him?

### 2. The official break — A board takes control
The formal record is clear. In a filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, NVIDIA said its board had accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer since 2013, was appointed interim chief executive while directors began looking for a permanent successor.

The board also appointed Harvey C. Jones as its chair.

That distinction matters. The company described the departure as a resignation, and the board accepted it. The public record does not justify casually rewriting that as a formal firing.

But the next chapter made clear that this was not a routine handover. Huang later said he would be willing to return if the board believed it was best for the company. Chairman Jones then said the board had unanimously accepted the resignation and did not intend to reinstate him.

For a founder accustomed to being the face and voice of NVIDIA, the decision was extraordinary. The board was asserting that the institution had authority beyond the individual who built it.

### 3. The crisis behind the headlines
The resignation followed a week of intense scrutiny over Huang's personal conduct and reports involving a woman described in media coverage as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset.

Those descriptions must be handled carefully. A reported suspicion is not proof of espionage, and an allegation is not a finding that classified information was transferred. NVIDIA's board said it was initiating an independent investigation after recent reporting that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort. The board also said it had no reason to believe that national security or NVIDIA secrets had been compromised.

Huang separately acknowledged serious mistakes in his personal life. The company and its board have not publicly established that any alleged intelligence operation succeeded.

The distinction is central to this story. A corporate crisis can be real even while some of the most dramatic claims remain unresolved. What is documented is that the board opened an investigation, the CEO resigned, and the company moved into a leadership transition.

### 4. Why the chips matter
This story reaches far beyond the private life of one executive because NVIDIA's products sit at the center of a strategic contest.

Its advanced processors are used to train and run powerful AI systems. They are valuable commercial products, but they are also strategically sensitive technology. Governments have imposed export controls to restrict China's access to certain advanced computing chips.

That makes the questions facing NVIDIA unusually consequential. What controls govern where its most advanced products can go? How does the company monitor customers, intermediaries and end users? And how should a global technology supplier balance commercial opportunity with national-security restrictions?

U.S. lawmakers have raised questions about whether restricted chips reached prohibited actors and about the possible role of a suspected intelligence asset. Those are questions for investigators and officials to establish, not conclusions this film can declare.

The House committees announced that Huang had agreed to a transcribed interview on December first, with NVIDIA general counsel Tim Teter scheduled for December second. The committees said they wanted answers about the company's role in a potential national-security breach.

The hearings could become a major test of what NVIDIA knew, what it did, and how the export-control system works in practice.

### 5. The return that did not happen
After leaving, Huang publicly said he had heard calls from employees, investors, business partners and customers for him to return. He said he would be honored to do so if the board decided it was in the best interests of the company and its shareholders.

But the board's answer was no.

Chairman Harvey Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate him. That statement turned a resignation into a lasting separation, at least under the board's current position.

Online, support for Huang became part of the story. But the information environment also became polluted. Reuters Fact Check found that a viral image purporting to show hundreds of employees demanding the board's resignation was fabricated. A separate open letter supporting a fair hearing for Huang was real.

The difference matters. In a crisis involving a public company, false claims can move markets, distort employee sentiment and make it harder to understand what shareholders and workers actually believe.

### 6. The company beneath the founder
NVIDIA now faces a leadership challenge that is larger than filling one office. It must reassure customers that product roadmaps will continue, reassure investors that execution will not falter, and demonstrate that governance and compliance systems can withstand intense scrutiny.

Kress knows the company's finances and operations after more than a decade as CFO. But taking over during a crisis is different from managing a normal succession. The board must find a permanent leader while the company answers questions from lawmakers and regulators.

The business itself remains enormous. NVIDIA's latest reported results show that demand for its computing infrastructure has not simply disappeared with its founder's departure. That is an important counterweight to the drama: the company's factories, suppliers, software ecosystem, customers and engineering teams do not vanish when one executive leaves.

### 7. What happens next
The next milestones are concrete. The board must choose a permanent CEO. Investigators must establish what happened, and what did not. Congressional committees are scheduled to hear from Huang and NVIDIA's general counsel. The company must show that its export-control and compliance processes are credible.

There is also a deeper question for the technology industry. NVIDIA became one of the defining companies of the AI era under a founder whose personality became part of its brand. But a public company cannot depend on charisma alone. It needs durable systems, accountable leadership and controls that work even when the stakes are high.

Jensen Huang built NVIDIA into an institution with global influence. Now that institution faces a test of its own.

The question is no longer only whether NVIDIA can succeed without its founder on stage.

It is whether the company can convince the world that its governance is as powerful as its technology.
