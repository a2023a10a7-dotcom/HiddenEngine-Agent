# Hidden Engine — Editorial Style Guide
## Project: China's Technology Revolution

This documentary uses the channel-wide creative identity established from the BlackBerry reference film. The topic changes; the visual grammar remains consistent.

### Brand system
- Backgrounds: near-black `#0A0A0B`, charcoal `#121212`–`#1C1C1E`.
- Primary text: `#FFFFFF`; secondary text: `#E5E5E5`.
- Quiet/deactivated elements: `#7A7A80`.
- Single accent: copper `#C86D2D` / `#D97724`.
- No additional competing accent colors.
- Typography: DejaVu Sans as the available production-safe geometric sans substitute; bold uppercase chapter titles with restrained tracking; regular-weight compact labels.
- Chapter markers: small copper eyebrow, short title, fine copper rule; avoid paragraph-length on-screen copy.
- Subtitles: white, centered in the lower safe area, dark outline/backing, unobtrusive.

### Visual language
- Use relevant, distinct images for each narration beat. Do not reuse an image simply to fill time.
- Prefer identifiable Chinese industrial context: factories, ports, logistics, electronics supply chains, EV assembly, battery production, solar manufacturing, robotics, rail, drones, semiconductor facilities and workers.
- Stock imagery is illustrative unless the depicted company, place, product or event is independently verified. Never present generic stock as documentary proof of a named company or event.
- No watermarks, low-resolution assets, irrelevant images or misleading visual substitutions.
- Use maps, timelines, clean comparisons and restrained data graphics when they explain a real claim. Cite the underlying source in credits and on the graphic where appropriate.
- Preserve dark negative space and high contrast. Apply slow, subtle Ken Burns movement to stills; use gentle fades/cross-dissolves rather than flashy transitions.

### Editorial and pacing
- English narration for a Western audience; measured, analytical, confident and story-led.
- Build the argument through a clear sequence: hook and paradox → industrial foundations → cluster economics → sector case studies → constraints and competitive pressure → implications and conclusion.
- Keep each visual beat tied to the exact narration passage. Let complex charts remain on screen long enough to read.
- Keep music atmospheric, restrained and clearly below narration. Sound effects should be sparse and soft.
- No sensational claims, unsupported superlatives or unverified statistics.

### Final delivery gate
- 1920×1080, 25 fps, H.264 MP4 with AAC audio.
- Confirm duration, audio stream, video stream, subtitle readability, contact sheet, visual credits and QA report.
- Review the full render, not only the contact sheet. Inspect opening, every chapter transition, charts, final minute and audio balance.
- Do not mark the video complete until technical QA and a human-style visual/audio review pass.
